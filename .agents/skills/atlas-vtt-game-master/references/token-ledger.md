## Purpose and decision procedure

A token stands for something the table has agreed it means. A portrait can identify a speaker; a figure can show physical position; a party emblem can identify a group; a chip can count a resource. Tabletop terminology overlaps across tokens, markers, counters and miniatures. In Atlas, an image token, note pin, drawing, condition badge and widget are different controls with different behavior.

1. Identify the immediate decision: who is present, where someone stands, what can be examined, what area is affected, or what value changed.
2. Choose the simplest adequate representation. Use narration or a note for information without useful spatial meaning. Use a pin for a destination or reference, a movable piece for independent position, and an existing numeric control for a quantity.
3. Establish meaning at this map scale. State whether the position is symbolic, approximate or physically consequential. Separate the subject's footprint from raster dimensions, transparent margins and selection size.
4. Check campaign rules, prior facts, visibility and state ownership. The ledger supplies options; it cannot establish a new condition, ownership right, progress trigger or travel cost.
5. Reuse a suitable asset. If the request includes creation, route by intended use to the owner listed below. Describe an unillustrated NPC by name when art would only postpone play.
6. Operate only within the authorized task. Record the proposal or application in the appropriate campaign document. Only actual play establishes an event or resource cost.

## People and groups: six uses

| Type | Use when | Suitable representation | Meaning to establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Player character | Identity or independent position matters | Named portrait for conversation; top-down figure for consequential positioning | Human controller, actual presence, footprint if relevant, and live resource owner |
| NPC | A recurring speaker or independently acting person needs a piece | Named portrait or figure; private preparation kept separately | GM controller; knowledge limits; introduction and departure follow fiction |
| Creature | An animal, monster or other being has a relevant position | Creature figure or cutout; linked card if rules need it | Identity, actual size, behavior and rules; artwork supplies no statistics |
| Companion or mount | Its movement, separation or safety creates a choice | Separate animal/companion piece; otherwise describe its presence | Who directs it, whether it carries anyone, and when it acts separately |
| Party or subgroup | Several people share a regional or city location | Shared emblem with membership in the session record | Who is together; a real split changes membership and location |
| Crowd, crew or formation | Treating several people collectively helps the decision | Group symbol or described area | Actual membership/count if known, extent and whether individuals can act separately; one symbol grants no formation bonuses |

Portraits communicate identity. They do not inherently show facing, reach, body size, hostility or ownership. Token overlap does not imply that subjects occupy exactly the same physical space at every scale. If individual movement becomes consequential, establish positions and use finer detail.

## Objects and places: eight uses

| Type | Use when | Suitable representation | Meaning to establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle | A cart, vessel or wagon moves or blocks something | Map prop with a stated footprint | Position, occupants, controller and movement rules; moving it does not automatically move passenger pieces |
| Portable item | A particular object can be found, handled or passed | Map prop, item image or note | Location and condition; actual acquisition, loss and custody require a recorded action |
| Container or cache | Its contents or access matter | Chest/crate/bag prop linked to the relevant note | Open/closed and known contents; a public name or preview must not disclose hidden contents |
| Furniture or obstacle | It can move independently or affect an approach | Reusable desk, beam or barricade prop | Physical extent, whether it can move, and adjudicated obstruction; a picture does not create collision |
| Movable terrain | A changing feature alters routes or access | Rock/tree/rubble prop or area drawing | What changed in the world, affected ground and known danger; a removed image alone does not clear a route |
| Mechanism | Someone can operate, inspect or disable it | Lever/winch/sluice prop plus operation note | Current state, accessible controls and known results; clicking art does not operate machinery |
| Landmark or destination | Players select a place at city or regional scale | Named location pin linked to a public hub | Symbolic position, existing routes and available detail; opening a link is presentation |
| Structure or settlement | A whole building or community matters at a broad scale | Roof/settlement symbol or location pin | Approximate regional extent and location; separately authored interiors establish precise geometry |

Keep useful fixed furniture in the background until independent manipulation matters. Before moving furniture depicted in the background, explain the authoritative changed layout; a new prop placed over it can otherwise suggest two desks. Native walls, doors, collision and lighting need their own supported configuration and verification.

## Information and effects: eight uses

| Type | Use when | Suitable representation | Meaning to establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence or clue | A discovered source needs inspection or reference | Revealed prop, numbered pin or handout | Observation versus interpretation; discovery state; private and public notes remain separate |
| Search location | Players identify where to look | Neutral named or numbered pin | The area they can search, not a promise that a secret or reward exists |
| Unidentified presence | Something is perceived without a known identity | Neutral silhouette, dot or generic marker | Exactly what was sensed; no hidden name, portrait, faction color or card preview |
| Objective | A current goal needs a shared reference | Destination marker or linked note | The agreed goal and completion terms; a marker creates no mandatory quest or reward |
| Route or waypoint | A plan, known path or actual journey benefits from display | Pins and a route drawing | Planned, followed or uncertain route; distance does not itself establish elapsed time |
| Hazard | Dangerous terrain or an affected area changes approaches | Area drawing plus a marker and stated stakes | Known extent, uncertainty, safe approaches and announced consequences |
| Effect area | A temporary effect has consequential coverage | Measured drawing/template if supported; prop for appearance | Shape, dimensions, duration and rule; do not derive a radius from decorative raster padding |
| Light source | Illumination or a carried source matters | Lantern/torch prop; native light where supported | Actual illumination rules and source state; a luminous image alone does not alter vision or fog |

Review public art, filenames, names, linked previews and descriptions for disclosure. Hiding a marker cannot remove a secret baked into the map raster. Essential clues follow campaign access rules; an absent token or failed search roll must not erase a guaranteed evidence route. A player-visible marker can show uncertainty without displaying the GM's answer.

## State and commitments: six uses

| Type | Use when | Suitable representation | Meaning to establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Condition | A defined state changes permitted actions or consequences | Configured badge or written condition | Exact campaign rule, subject, cause and ending event; no imported system effects by assumption |
| Resource | A quantity is gained, spent or restored | Existing actor resource field or counter | One live owner, range if defined, and actual earned change; currency/stock transfers require separate records |
| Progress or pressure | A defined event advances in observable steps | Clock/counter or note | Trigger, advancement and result; use only an agreed rule, and advance it manually unless verified automation exists |
| Turn or activation | A game needs action order | Initiative tracker or current-actor reminder | Who may act and what ends the turn; a conversation does not require combat initiative |
| Custody or control | Holding or controlling something changes choices | Session record; optional reminder symbol | Holder/controller, basis and actual transfer; physical possession and legal authority can differ |
| Promise, favor or obligation | A specific relationship commitment must return later | Linked note or short reminder card | Exact terms, parties, who knows and whether fulfilled; a general trust score cannot replace those facts |

Use numeric influence or reputation only if the campaign adopts such rules. Otherwise record what happened and what the person intends. Refusal to endorse an NPC may change assistance or testimony without deleting core evidence. NPCs react to information they plausibly received, not to every private player conversation.

## Minimum record for a consequential piece

Use existing asset metadata, notes and Session State. This is a compact example, not a new database or a form required for every portrait. Record fields that affect operation; leave unknown native IDs or readiness unconfirmed instead of inventing them.

```yaml
id: pia-doorstep                     # Logical campaign ID, not an invented native ID
kind: evidence-prop
subject: a reclaimed mooring stone used as a doorstep
representation: existing scene description; prop only if handling matters
scale: local; physical footprint not yet established
controller: GM for the object; player chooses hero interactions
visibility: public appearance; unrevealed interpretation stays private
state_owner: location note for the stone; Session State for actual custody
links: existing public location/evidence note if created; private prep separately
change_event: resolved inspection, alteration or agreed transfer
readiness: proposed; no image generated or Atlas placement performed
```

For quantities, identify the live scene/token/widget owner and any presentation copies. For precise geometry, record physical footprint, orientation and a checked scale. For commitments, record exact words or faithful terms, relevant witnesses and unresolved delivery. Use a stable logical ID when useful; record a native locator only after observing it. Do not add rules through metadata.

## Route art by intended use

Resolve these owners in the active Atlas repository's `.agents/skills/` or available skill catalog. Read the selected owner's current instructions when its work is actually requested. The purpose of an image, rather than its subject alone, determines the route.

| Intended deliverable | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| Establish a character's approved appearance | `atlas-vtt-portrait-forge` |
| Character/creature portrait token or top-down figure | `atlas-vtt-token-forge` |
| Furniture, terrain, structures, vehicles or objects on a map | `atlas-vtt-props-terrain-forge` |
| Item presented as treasure or equipment | `atlas-vtt-item-treasure-forge` |
| Party emblem, faction symbol or seal | `atlas-vtt-heraldry-symbol-forge` |
| Document, letter or discoverable writing | `atlas-vtt-handout-forge` |
| Playable region, settlement or interior | `atlas-vtt-map-forge` |
| Atmospheric location or moment | `atlas-vtt-scene-illustration-forge` |

For example, a sword on the floor is a prop; an equipment closeup belongs to Item & Treasure Forge. A group emblem need not pass through character Token Forge. Reuse approved identity references for recurring people. A generically described person can remain without art. Do not commission portraits for every person solely to complete the catalog.

Separate desired world footprint from requested raster size and actual native display size. Review transparency, silhouette, crop, viewpoint, readability and information disclosure where relevant. A token circle is an identity frame, not a creature's diameter. A prop's cast shadow can rotate with its image and should not be treated as world lighting.

## Operate, verify and resume

Read the current campaign checkpoint and affected token/setup or location note before a consequential change. Check whether the piece already exists, who is actually present, and what has already happened. Use the intended vault and scene. Keep one native operator; a proposed cast does not authorize multiple operators to populate it.

Use the current native Atlas controls. Inspect target identity before moving, hiding, renaming or spending. A context menu on overlapping pieces does not prove which piece was selected. Saved encounters are spawn templates, not fictional arrivals or a rule that everyone must appear. Place manually when a formation is unverified; do not claim automated positioning works because a cast saved successfully.

Match verification to the actual change:

| Claim | Relevant evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Design saved | The actual Markdown/spec and its resolved references |
| Image usable | Inspected image, actual dimensions/alpha when relevant, and readability at its intended display |
| Imported | Native asset exists and resolves to the intended image |
| Placed | Correct scene, identity and observed position; precise footprint checked if consequential |
| Player-safe | Fresh Player View shows intended names/art and excludes unrevealed content |
| Resource change applied | Correct live owner's observed value plus the actual resolved cost |
| Persistence verified | Relevant close/reopen observation; a saved field is separate corroboration |

Check only what the task needs. Do not turn ordinary play into an acceptance tour, generate a new map to answer a conversation question, or claim untested lighting, collision, resource synchronization or scripted triggers. Describe limited support honestly and continue through narration when exact native geometry is unnecessary.

If an operation fails, inspect whether any effect already happened before retrying. Record confirmed fiction separately from a native effect that is unapplied or uncertain. On resume, reconcile the intended scene and owner with fresh evidence; do not duplicate a piece, reveal, roll or spend. A copied token/card is not automatically a new live resource pool.

Filesystem tools can write ordinary campaign Markdown. Use native controls for native state; do not hand-edit `.atlasmap`, `.atlas-data` or plugin configuration. A scene snapshot does not restore campaign notes, all collection settings, or the whole game's history. Consult the checkpoint before any restoration.

These are behavioral instructions. They do not add tool access, enforced permissions, a rules engine or automatic synchronization. Tool availability and authorization come from the active runtime and user request.

## Worked applications

### Existing Tide Witness regional party convention

The regional emblem represents the people currently traveling together in a six-mile area. Several characters can share a hex. The city emblem identifies their city location; local portraits identify people and approximate positions inside the office. Read the live campaign Token Setup and Session State before using their exact owners or memberships.

If the heroes split, record subgroup membership and actual destinations before introducing separate markers. If they regroup, resolve that event and reconcile the markers. A display adjustment, scene tab change or portrait rearrangement does not establish travel or spend Delay.

### Proposed threshold stone investigation

The doorstep is initially description, with visible wear when inspected. A prop becomes useful if the heroes handle or relocate it. A marker linked to its revealed evidence can support later reference. Keep Oren's interpretation separate from the observation. Pia's permission, any paid work and a promised copy belong in actual session records only when agreed in play.

If players propose transferring the stone to an archive, establish Pia's consent, replacement needs and the actual handling before showing a new location. Moving the prop alone establishes none of those things. This example is proposed material, not an assertion that Pia, a new asset or that agreement already exists.

### Existing resources across presentation copies

In Tide Witness, original regional hero tokens own live Strain/Kit and the regional counter owns Delay. Local portraits carry presentation only. A hypothetical confirmed Kit spend while the office is displayed is applied once at the correct original owner; Player View can stay on the office. If the control fails after the action resolves, record the earned cost and uncertain application, then verify before trying again.

### Generic uncertain sighting and hazard

If the heroes see movement across reeds without identifying it, use a neutral marker labeled only with perceived information. Keep the actual identity and private card off Player View. If dangerous ground has known boundaries, draw that area and state the observed warning. If its extent is uncertain, describe that uncertainty; decorative art must not imply a surveyed safe edge.

### Proposed promise and later consequence

If Ren promises Pia a copy and Oren hears, record the terms and witnesses. A reminder can read "Copy owed" if that is player-known. At the next relevant encounter, Pia can ask for it and Oren can act on what he heard. No automatic reputation penalty, currency loss or Delay follows unless the campaign's rules or announced consequences establish it.

## Authority and sources

Campaign Rules establish mechanics; the Play Agreement assigns player/GM control; Session State records actual events; maintained location and NPC preparation establish geography and private possibilities. Read those live documents when applying this general knowledge. Current native behavior requires the affected source or installation evidence rather than assuming every ledger row is an Atlas feature.

The taxonomy above is a practical GM synthesis. It is not a copied game system or a catalog of native Atlas asset classes. These primary sources illustrate the broader tabletop distinction, checked 2026-10-04:

- [D&D Playing the Game](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/playing-the-game): miniatures or tokens represent creature positions under that game's grid rules. Those size, movement and combat rules do not automatically apply to Tide Witness.
- [Fate Core: Fate Points](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/fate-points): physical tokens can represent a spendable resource. This illustrates a use; it does not add Fate Points to the current campaign.
